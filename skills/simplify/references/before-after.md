# Worked pairs

Read these before you flatten anything. The move is easier to copy than to describe.

Each pair keeps every claim from the original. Nothing is dropped to make a sentence shorter. Where
the original hid a number, the flattened version flags it rather than inventing one.

---

## 1. The governing thought

**L5 — as written**

> Our strategic ambition is to become the leading omnichannel partner for premium personal care,
> leveraging our physical footprint as a differentiator while accelerating digital-first customer
> acquisition across key European markets.

**L1 — flattened**

> We sell in shops and online. Right now the shops make the money.
> We want the website to make money too.
> We think our shops are the reason people trust us online.
> That belief is the whole plan. Nobody has tested it.

What happened: "leveraging our physical footprint as a differentiator" is an assumption pretending to
be a fact. Flattening exposes it. The last two lines are not editorialising — they are the claim,
stated plainly.

---

## 2. A number

**L5**

> We aim to grow our share of the premium segment from 12% to 18% over the plan period.

**L1**

> Of every 100 people who buy premium care, 12 buy it from us.
> We want that to be 18.
> That means winning 6 more out of every 100. [TO FILL: by which date]

What happened: the percentage got a comparison, the delta got named, and "the plan period" got
flagged instead of guessed. "Over the plan period" is not a date.

---

## 3. A recommendation with no sacrifice

**L5**

> We will double down on our core categories while continuing to explore adjacent opportunities and
> maintaining our presence in emerging markets.

**L1**

> We put our money into the three things we already sell most of.
> Which means we stop putting money into the other eleven.
> The plan does not say that. It says we keep everything. [TO FILL: which categories lose funding]

What happened: the opposite test kills this sentence. No company chooses *not* to focus on its core.
The flattened version says what a real version of this choice would cost, and flags that the document
never made it.

---

## 4. A frozen verb and a hidden actor

**L5**

> A comprehensive review of the operating model will be undertaken to ensure organisational readiness
> for the transformation.

**L1**

> Someone will look at how we work today. [TO FILL: who, and by when]
> The reason is that nobody knows if we can actually do this.

What happened: "a review will be undertaken" hides both the actor and the worry underneath it. The
worry is the useful part.

---

## 5. A contradiction between two documents

**L5 — HR annual plan**

> Headcount will remain flat through the plan period as we drive efficiency through automation.

**L5 — Commercial strategy**

> The direct sales motion will be scaled significantly to support the enterprise push.

**L1**

> HR says the number of people stays the same.
> Sales says we need a lot more sales people.
> Both are in the plan. Only one can happen.

What happened: neither document is wrong on its own. The flattened version puts them next to each
other, which is the only place the problem is visible. Never soften this into "there is some tension
between the plans".

---

## 6. A confidence the source did not have

**L5**

> Market signals suggest that demand for sustainable formulations will continue its upward trajectory.

**L1**

> We think people will keep wanting greener products.
> We are guessing. The plan does not say what we looked at. [TO FILL: which market data]

What happened: "market signals suggest" is a vague source. It is not evidence, and flattening must
not turn it into evidence. "We think" stays "we think".

---

## 7. The three seats

**L5**

> Key priorities include margin recovery, digital acceleration, supply chain resilience, talent
> retention, portfolio rationalisation and sustainability leadership.

**L1**

> Three things have to be solved. The plan lists six.
>
> 1. We are not making enough money per sale.
> 2. Our website does not sell enough.
> 3. We cannot get stock where it needs to be.
>
> The other three matter. They are not what this year is about.

What happened: six priorities is no priorities. Forcing three makes the ranking visible, and saying
plainly that the other three were demoted is more honest than dropping them silently.
