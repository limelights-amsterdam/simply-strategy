# The words

Someone who knows nothing reads this page on a phone and understands it without help. Every sentence
has to earn that.

## Rules

- One idea per sentence. Short enough to read aloud in one breath.
- One name per thing, the whole page through. Use the material's own noun if it has one. If the
  deck says "in-home services", the page says "in-home services" on every screen, never "home
  products" on one and "household services" on the next.
- Keep the material's jargon only where it is the name of a thing, and put it in quotation marks
  the first time: "DBSS", "BPX". Translate everything else. "Structural rationalisation" becomes
  "what stops".
- Verbs, not nouns. Stops, grows, moves, owns. Not "reduction", "transformation", "enablement".
- Present tense. Active voice. Say who does it when the material says who.
- Write to the reader. "The deck says", not "the deck states that".
- Vary sentence length. A four word sentence next to a long one is how a person sounds.
- Write in English, whatever language the material is in. Keep a title or a quote in its own
  language when it is the name of the thing.

## Never

<!-- plainlint-ignore-start -->

- Em dashes. Use a comma, a colon or a full stop.
- Adverbs and intensifiers: fundamentally, significantly, truly, very.
- Threes for the sake of threes. If the material names four things, name four.
- Marketing adjectives: seamless, robust, cutting-edge, world-class. Put the fact there instead.
- Office verbs: leverage, drive, enable, align, unlock, navigate, double down.
- Announcements: "here's the thing", "let that sink in", "this matters because".
- A closing paragraph of optimism. The page ends on the gaps and the sources.
- Title case in headings. A headline of more than one sentence (a second sentence is a second claim:
  push it under the picture or give it its own section). Emoji. Exclamation marks.
- Confidence the material did not have. "We think" stays "we think". "Indicative" stays on the page,
  next to the figure it qualifies.

## The material's own sentences

When the material has a sentence that already does the job, use it, in the italic serif, with its
source. "Incremental optimisation is no longer sufficient to remain competitive" beats any
paraphrase, because it is theirs.
