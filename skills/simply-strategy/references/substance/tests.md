# The seven tests, in full

Use this file when reviewing. Each test gives its origin, the exact question you ask, and what a successful repair looks like.

They run hardest first. If a document dies on test 1, you can skip 4 through 7: there is no claim left to test.

## 1. The opposite test

**Origin.** Roger Martin uses this to separate strategy from policy. If the reverse of your claim is absurd, you have not taken a position.

**The question.** Could a sensible competitor claim the reverse and get away with it?

**How to run it.** Put the claim literally into the negative. Read it aloud. If it sounds like something nobody would ever say, the original claim is empty.

| Claim | Reversed | Could a competitor say this? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We deliver high quality | We deliver low quality | No, empty |
| We listen to our customers | We ignore our customers | No, empty |
| We are innovative | We are conservative | Only if you say in what, vague |
| We deliver within 24 hours, at a higher price | We deliver in a week, cheaper | Yes, real choice |
| We build everything in-house | We buy everything in | Yes, real choice |

**Repair.** Add who, what, how much or where until the reversal becomes defensible. Usually that means naming the segment, the number, or the alternative you are rejecting.

**Follow-up questions.**
- Who would disagree with this, and why are they wrong?
- Which competitor does the opposite, and does it work for them?
- If this holds for everyone in the industry, what part of it is ours?

## 2. The sacrifice test

**Origin.** Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy*: bad strategy is recognisable by the absence of a choice. Michael Porter put it shorter: strategy is choosing what not to do.

**The question.** What falls away because of this?

**How to run it.** Search the document for "not", "stop", "wind down", "no longer" in combination with an activity, market or customer. If you cannot find one, nothing has been given up.

Watch for false sacrifices. "We say no to what does not fit our strategy" sacrifices nothing, because it names no actual project.

| Type | Example | Does it count? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real | We close the Rotterdam office | Yes |
| Real | We decline deals below 25k | Yes |
| False | We say no to what does not fit | No |
| False | We focus more sharply | No |
| False | We prioritise better | No |

**Repair.** Ask: which customer calls next year and gets turned down? Which product stops getting maintenance? Which department gets no extra budget? One concrete answer is enough to save the document.

**Follow-up questions.**
- What did we do last year that we will not do next year?
- Who inside the organisation gets angry about this? If nobody does, nothing was chosen.
- How much revenue are we willing to lose?

## 3. The falsification test

**Origin.** From philosophy of science, by way of the pre-mortem in strategy work. A claim that cannot be wrong steers nothing.

**The question.** Which number, on which date, proves this was wrong?

**How to run it.** For each objective look for three things: a quantity, a number and a date. If one is missing, the objective is not testable.

| Objective | Quantity | Number | Date | Testable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| We strengthen our position | none | none | none | No |
| We grow substantially | revenue | none | none | No |
| We grow 20 percent | revenue | 20% | none | Almost |
| Revenue is 4.2m on 1 Jan 2027 | revenue | 4.2m | 1 Jan 2027 | Yes |

**The stop threshold.** Stronger than a target is a stop threshold: the number below which you quit. It forces the conversation that otherwise arrives a year late. Ask for it, even when the document did not.

**Repair.** Add quantity, number and date. If the organisation cannot name the number, that is itself the finding: the decision rests on a feeling. That is allowed, but then write it that way.

**Follow-up questions.**
- When will we know this is not working?
- What is the worst result we would still continue with?
- Who measures this, and which system does the figure come from?

## 4. The mechanism test

**Origin.** The core of Rumelt's "kernel": diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action. Most documents jump from diagnosis straight to target and skip the policy.

**The question.** How exactly does A cause B?

**How to run it.** Find the sentence where growth or improvement is promised. Count the steps in between. Zero steps means a miracle is required.

- ❌ We grow through better marketing. *(zero steps)*
- ⚠️ We grow through more online visibility. *(one step, still no buyer in view)*
- ✅ Buyers at mid-size construction firms search by part number, not by brand name. They currently land at the wholesaler. We publish a page per part. That catches the traffic before the wholesaler sees it. *(buyer, behaviour, intervention, result)*

**What you want to see in the chain.** Who the buyer is. What they do now. Why they would switch. What we must build for that. Why the competitor does not copy it immediately.

That last one is the most commonly skipped. A mechanism anyone can replicate tomorrow explains no advantage.

**Follow-up questions.**
- Who pays, and for what exactly?
- Why are they not buying from us already?
- What stops the competitor from taking this over?

## 5. The so-what test

**Origin.** Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*. Every level of an argument must carry the level above it.

**The question.** And therefore?

**How to run it.** Put the word "so" after every factual sentence in the document. If the sentence does not continue, the fact is loose gravel.

| Observation | So what |
| --- | --- |
| The market grew 12 percent | We grew 3 percent, so we are losing share |
| We are losing share | It traces back to how long our quotes take |
| Our quote takes 4 hours | So below 25k order value we lose money |
| (no so-what) | So we stop accepting those deals |

Four observations stacked on each other produce one decision. That is an argument. Four loose observations produce a "market analysis" chapter nobody reads.

**Repair.** Cut every fact you cannot connect to a decision. A strategy document is not a report: everything in it must carry the choice.

**The reverse also holds.** Every conclusion must rest on something stated earlier. A decision that appears without support is as bad as a fact without consequence.

## 6. The ownership test

**The question.** Who, by when, and what does done look like?

**How to run it.** Walk the action list. An action is complete with a person's name, a date, and a visible end result.

| Action | Owner | Done by | Done means | Complete |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pick up market research | The team | Q3 | none | No |
| Search term analysis | Sanne | 15 Sep | Report with volume per term | Yes |

"The team", "the organisation", "we", and a quarter instead of a date are the four most common ways to let an action evaporate.

**Follow-up questions.**
- Who makes the call if this is not finished on 16 September?
- Does this person have the time and the mandate, or are they already on four other rows?

---

## 7. The already-true test

**The question.** What changes on Monday that was not true on Friday?

**How to run it.** Read the document as an outsider and ask, per decision, whether it describes the current state of affairs. Many strategy documents are a tidy description of what was happening anyway, with a year printed above it.

- ❌ We continue to invest in our people. *(you already did)*
- ❌ We are committed to digitalisation. *(running for three years)*
- ✅ We bring the service desk in-house on 1 October and drop the vendor.

**Repair.** Ask for the difference. If there is none, the document is an account of the past, not a strategy. That is fine, but it needs a different title.

## What you do with the outcome

Close a review with one line that places the document. That distinction steers the conversation better than a score:

| Verdict | When | What it is good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strategy** | A choice with a sacrifice and a point of reckoning, and actions with an owner and a date | Can be decided on and run |
| **Strategy without execution** | The choice is there, the actions are not | Can be decided on, cannot yet be run |
| **Plan** | Actions with owners and dates, but no choice underneath | Can be executed, steers nothing |
| **Direction** | A sacrifice or a number, not both | Can inspire, cannot decide |
| **Wish** | No sacrifice, no number, no owner | Back to the drawing board |

`stratlint.py` places a document on the same ladder from five yes/no questions: is there a sacrifice,
a number, a date, an owner, a stop threshold. It sees that a choice was made, not whether it was
the right one.

Be honest here. A document that is a "direction" does not become a strategy because you filled in the tables. Someone still has to choose, and that is not your job but the reader's. The most useful thing you can do is point at exactly which choice is still open.
