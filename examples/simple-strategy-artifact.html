Simple strategy artifact22 August 2026 · vision-2030

The plan cuts 500 people, and the work that must grow needs them.

One deck, seven slides. It promises a cut of about 500 people. It calls a security step-up non-negotiable. It names two growth pillars. The same staff sit under all three. Here is what that means, one picture at a time.

How this was decided

The deck states the cut on slide 6 and the growth pillars on slide 3.1 Neither slide mentions the other.

Four angles read it blind. Three reached the same collision by different routes: substance through the ownership test, contradiction by pairing slides 4 and 3, and the Compass angle by reading it against the boundary the client set.

The fourth, red team, produced findings that duplicated the contradiction angle, so the plan agent weighted them down rather than counting them twice.

Three things must be solved

Each one has a number missing, a name missing, or both.

1

The cut is counted two ways, and it takes the cheap people.

Slide 6 gives two sizes for one cut.1 Slide 4 removes the cheapest staff and keeps the dearest.2 the unit can reach 900 on time and buy the hands back as contractors.

How this was decided

Found by the substance angle on the falsification test: two numbers for one commitment cannot both be the target.

The contradiction angle reached it separately and ranked it first of nine by cost if nobody notices.

Two of three reviewers marked the first wording fatal, because it stated the gap without naming which figure is the plan of record.

2

The one thing that cannot slip has no price on it.

The deck calls the step-up non-negotiable.1 No slide gives it a cost, a scope, an owner or a date.

  • No amount, anywhere in the deck
  • No owner named
  • No date between today and 2030
  • The works council must advise first, which is our reading of Dutch law

The deck does not price the step-up, so this one gets no figure. An empty graphic would be worse than none.

How this was decided

The Compass names the step-up as a boundary the client will not trade.4 The deck names it non-negotiable and never returns to it.

All four angles quoted the same sentence here. That is one finding, not four, and the plan agent counted it once, which is why it sits second rather than first.

3

Both growth bets run on the labour the plan removes.

In-home service means someone walks into a house. Slide 5 answers with cheaper integrators.3 You can buy hands, not the promise slide 4 makes.2

How this was decided

The Compass angle found this by reading the anti-vision: the client described a 2030 where rivals deliver at a lower unit cost, and this plan removes the people who deliver.

The red-team angle reached the same place from outside, asking what a rival does on Monday. Its version was ruled a duplicate.

What we stop doing

The deck names four real stops. A rival could sensibly keep all four.

Stops
  • Email, on-prem video, Digitenne1
  • Manned channels and channel software2
  • Field engineers, L1 and L2 roles2
  • Netflix stays non-white-label1
Starts
  • A security step-up1
  • Security and in-home as growth1
  • Selling to smaller SME customers1
  • More work bought from integrators3

Under the three must-solves, nothing stops. The deck asks for a different seniority mix and never says which grades leave.1

What has to be true

Six bets. The deck states none of them as a bet.

The heaviest bet

900 people hold reliability end to end, without L1 and L2.2

How we would test it

Name who answers at 03:00 when an integrator breaks it.

How this was decided

Six assumptions were extracted by the substance angle. The test column is ours: no document proposes a test for any of them.

Two cannot be tested at all yet, because whether security slips or the 900 slips depends on who holds a veto, and the Compass does not name the room.4

What the documents disagree about

Nine pairs in one deck cannot both be true.

Slide 4 says

IT takes end-to-end responsibility for reliability, security and continuity.2

Slide 5 says

Increased use of lower-cost system integrators.3

You cannot hand out the work and keep the answer. That is the costliest of the nine. The other eight are in the reasoning log, with both sides quoted.

  • One deck says tech grows and shrinks1,3
  • Integrator hours are people cost, and land in OPEX1,3
  • A subscription is OPEX, not CAPEX1,3
  • The reason for the hurry names no competitor and no unit cost4
How this was decided

The contradiction angle was the only one reading every document at once, and found nine.

It ranked them by what it costs if nobody says it out loud, not by how obvious they are. The one above is first because the money follows the integrators while the promise stays with IT.

What we do not know

Eight of these stop a decision. The rest stop the work.

Blocks the decision

Who is in the room, and who is hostile to this.4

Blocks the decision

What the step-up costs, and whether it sits inside the 900.

Blocks the decision

Whether 1400 counts the unit IT or all of the unit.

Blocks the decision

Any money at all. The deck names no amount.

How this was decided

Eleven gaps were carried from the spec and the four angles. Nothing here is an estimate: each line is missing from the source rather than guessed.

The split between blocking a decision and blocking the work is the plan agent’s, made when it forced the ranking to three.
